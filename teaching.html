Teaching

Teaching students how to learn is just as important as teaching them what to learn.

My goal is to give students the confidence to tackle unfamiliar problems and think like scientists. Whether I am teaching memory, statistics, or mentoring a student researcher, I try to make the reasoning behind science visible: not just what we know, but how we ask questions, evaluate evidence, and arrive at conclusions.

Making the Research Process Visible

I break scientific thinking into steps that students can practise, connect, and eventually carry out independently.

ASK

How does curiosity become a research question?

I help students move from an interesting observation to a question that can actually be tested.

DESIGN

How can an experiment answer that question?

Students learn that methodological choices determine what a study can—and cannot—tell us.

INTERPRET

What do the data actually mean?

I teach students to use analysis to evaluate evidence, rather than treating statistics as procedures to memorize.

COMMUNICATE

How does a result become a scientific argument?

Students practise connecting evidence to conclusions and communicating those ideas clearly to different audiences.
